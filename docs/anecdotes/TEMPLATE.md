# Anecdote intake form

Copy this file into `docs/anecdotes/inbox/` and name it `YYYY-MM-short-title.md`. Fill it in. Two minutes, a few lines per box. Rough is fine; the tools do the rest.

> **Rules before you write anything.**
> No real names, countries, cities, dates or titles of real people.
> Nothing ongoing, nothing classified, nothing that identifies a private individual.
> Write "an ambassador", "a junior colleague", "a neighbouring country", "a ministry". If you catch yourself typing a name, use a role instead.
> The game disguises the story further. Your job is only to give it the raw material.

## 1. What happened

(2-5 lines. Facts in order. No names.)

## 2. Why it was absurd

(One or two lines. What made people in the room freeze, laugh later, or write a note to file?)

## 3. Who was involved (roles only)

(Example: "a head of delegation, a very junior note-taker, an interpreter, a caterer.")

## 4. What happened next

(The outcome, the cover-up, the formal follow-up, the thing nobody mentioned again.)

## 5. Rough era

(1980s / around 2001 / 2006 / 2013 / 2022 / any)

## 6. Where it might fit

(Chapter or rank if you know: intern, junior officer, mid-career, ambassador. "No idea" is fine. Also say if it feels like a mail, a conversation, a newspaper item or a full scene.)

## 7. What must be disguised

(Anything someone could use to recognise the event: the object, the venue, the sequence, a phrase, a nickname. List it so the tools change it.)

## Filled-in example (fictional)

**1. What happened.** A working lunch at a multilateral meeting. Delegations had agreed the seating by email over three weeks. On the day, a delegate found the card with his country's name on the wrong side of the table, next to a delegation he was not speaking to. He moved the card. The next delegate moved his. By the soup course, nine cards had moved and the chair announced that the seating plan was "a living document".

**2. Why it was absurd.** Everyone moved cards for a different reason. Nobody admitted moving one. The version number of the plan was higher than the number of guests.

**3. Who was involved.** A chair, nine delegates, a protocol officer, a waiter who had been told the original plan by phone.

**4. What happened next.** The protocol officer issued a corrected plan after the lunch, showing the seating as it had occurred and recording it as the intended arrangement.

**5. Rough era.** Any.

**6. Where it might fit.** Mid-career, a coordination meeting. A full scene with three choices.

**7. What must be disguised.** The real meeting type, the cheese course (people remember it), the phrase "living document" if it is a known catchphrase of one person.
