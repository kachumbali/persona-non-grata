# EU coordination ideas — review draft

## Scope

These are adapted candidates from the supplied Gemini material, for review before implementation. They take place in and around the EU Delegation, Port Hesperia, the host ministry and diplomatic receptions: everyday local diplomatic coordination, not a gathering of national leaders. Summit-specific material is excluded. The chapter mission and daily side stories continue in parallel; none of these ideas is required to complete the Minister's visit.

Preserve the game's established fictional host Ombrazia, Port Hesperia and existing fictional third countries. Treat the submitted host-country names as placeholders, not a request to rename the game world. Use real names for EU states and fictional people; keep other third countries fictional and non-obvious. Use "EU" in player-facing text. Bellaluxe's 17.6% is the fictional premise established in the game, not a historical claim about any real state.

## The 17.6% running gag

Each independent introduction should make the premise clear: Bellaluxe contributes 17.6% of the EU's Multiannual Financial Framework, and some of its diplomats try to apply that percentage to unrelated things. These are story premises, not fixed calculations for unrelated game systems.

1. **The biscuit tray:** At a local EU coordination coffee, a Bellaluxe colleague calculates the country's precise share of pastries. Defend the claim, share the food, or rebrand the last pastry as a joint initiative. Other missions may remember the generosity or the arithmetic.
2. **Speaking time:** During a local meeting, a representative measures who has spoken and demands Bellaluxe's budget-proportional share. Protest, speak faster, or let a smaller represented mission use the time. Adapt the suggested interpretation-booth scene to the delegation's actual meeting scale rather than assuming elaborate conference infrastructure.
3. **A safety drill:** During a local port exercise, an attaché applies the budget share to seats on a practice raft. Insist on a fractional place, board promptly, or submit a note after the drill. The host's safety team and other missions react to the choice. Avoid turning a practical emergency into a compulsory gag.
4. **The toner cartridge:** A shared coordination office is short of printer supplies. Buy more, demand an exact share, or pool the remaining stock. A later stationery feud can depend on what the player did; real financial shares for other countries are not specified.
5. **The project plaque:** Bellaluxe disputes the size of its crest on a local cooperation project's plaque. Ask for a correction, make a smaller symbolic protest, or accept the design. The host's time and goodwill matter as well as domestic visibility.
6. **The empty coffee pot:** At a late local coordination meeting, diplomats try to assign coffee and credit by contribution. Prioritise Bellaluxe, share quietly, or broker a jointly branded pot. Show a follow-up response rather than making any one outcome automatically correct.

The submitted commemorative-medal idea is on hold because the EU received the Nobel Peace Prize in 2012, after the roughly 2006 setting. It can be rewritten as an invented EU exhibit or kept as another deliberate anachronism if the user wants it. [Nobel Prize record](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/peace/2012/summary/)

## Adapted side-story candidates

### Representation of absent missions

- **Customs clause:** Two absent missions have given a resident diplomat instructions on a local tracking proposal. The player can defend the instructions, seek permission for a compromise or make a Bellaluxe-only demand. If the missions disagree, document their different instructions before the player chooses; do not claim that one "secretly wanted" the opposite while giving identical instructions without explaining the mismatch.
- **Small-boat exemptions:** Two represented missions seek exemptions while another prioritises industrial vessels. Defend the small-boat position, negotiate a trade, or ask the Delegation to coordinate. A successful defence can be a rare, funny diplomatic win; other affected missions still react.
- **Infrastructure funding:** Represented states prefer a secure communications project while another mission wants port-tracking infrastructure. Advocate for the authorised position, seek new instructions or propose a split. Being entrusted to speak for someone has a relationship consequence if their instructions are ignored.

Estonia, Latvia and Slovenia are EU member states in the around-2006 setting. Treat procedural tools as story choices appropriate to the local meeting; do not promise the player a legal veto merely because a proposed dialogue says so.

### Host-country access and protocol

- **Reception seating:** Major missions receive prominent seats while smaller ones are placed beside the kitchen. Accept and network, challenge protocol, or attempt a nameplate switch. Develop this as a distinct reception or connect it to the existing seating-plan arc instead of duplicating that arc.
- **Port-security briefing:** The host minister invites only the largest missions; smaller missions and the EU Delegation wait outside. Request access, work with the Delegation head for a broader session, or seek information from a junior contact. Access, trust and the host's view should respond to the approach.
- **Waterfront project:** Smaller-mission staff spot a planning issue that larger partners missed. Use it to negotiate recognition, pass it to the Delegation, or alert the host directly. A host official may value the useful warning even if their office overlooked Bellaluxe initially.

Existing Director Ndao and Minister Bako can carry appropriate roles where they fit; otherwise introduce a new fictional host official with their own motives. Do not replace established characters simply to copy draft names.

### Receptions

- **Finnish midsummer reception:** A hot warehouse and over-formal guests make an informal midsummer event unexpectedly difficult. Finland's Midsummer is not its Independence Day; if the event is specifically a national-day reception, use 6 December instead. [ThisisFINLAND holiday calendar](https://finland.fi/facts-stats-and-info/the-finnish-flag/)
- **Portuguese reception:** A long cultural performance competes with the chance to meet maritime-insurance contacts. A colleague may be a better or worse representative depending on who they are; do not hard-code every delegate to fall asleep.
- **Belgian reception:** A culinary debate opens a route to useful budget-alignment information. Attending or sending an appropriate colleague can lead to a later informal brunch; declining need not be punished by default.
- **Austrian reception:** Formal dancing in a humid room creates a protocol problem and an opportunity for a capable colleague. Offer a polite, non-dancing way to engage as well as attendance, delegation or regrets.

Dates are authored according to the final in-game timeline. Preserve the player's ability to attend, delegate or decline where appropriate; missed, declined and delegated must remain distinguishable. Consequences should follow the individual and the choice rather than automatically rewarding personal attendance.

### Joint credit dispute: clean-port initiative

The Delegation head asks member states to contribute staff and funds to a local clean-port initiative, while the draft announcement centres the Delegation's role. Three approaches:

1. **Accept the draft:** secure cooperation quickly, but Bellaluxe may be overlooked and Capital may object.
2. **Insist on national branding:** protect visibility, but risk delay, disagreement and the Delegation head's trust.
3. **Negotiate shared credit:** agree a practical division of work and visibility, potentially using the 17.6% joke. It improves the chance of cooperation but still has a cost, a compromise or a party who is not fully satisfied.

Resolve actual staff time and money through the existing planning rules. Let the host react based on what was agreed and who did the work; no automatic perfect outcome. A later local event or the Minister's visit can acknowledge the choice. The side story remains optional and cannot make the chapter impossible by itself.
