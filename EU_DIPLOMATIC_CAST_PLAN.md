# EU diplomatic cast and satire plan — draft for review

## Aim

Make EU coordination a busy, playable social ecosystem in the setting around 2006. The chapter mission (the Minister's visit) and the daily diplomatic stories continue in parallel. EU meetings, receptions and development initiatives create choices, relationships and consequences; they must not become mandatory to complete the chapter.

## Period and deliberate departures

- The game is set around 2006, after ten countries joined in May 2004 and before Bulgaria and Romania joined in 2007. The historical EU had 25 members in 2006. [EU enlargement history](https://european-union.europa.eu/principles-countries-history/eu-enlargement_en)
- The 2006 members were Austria, Belgium, Cyprus, Czechia, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Hungary, Ireland, Italy, Latvia, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Malta, the Netherlands, Poland, Portugal, Slovakia, Slovenia, Spain, Sweden and the United Kingdom. In player-facing text use **EU**, not a numbered label.
- The BlockBerry, fax and desktop-based communication are central to the 2006 feel. Apple introduced the first iPhone in January 2007. [Apple announcement](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2007/01/09Apple-Reinvents-the-Phone-with-iPhone/)
- "Team Europe" as an official approach dates from 2020; Commission delegations were renamed EU delegations after the Lisbon Treaty, and the 2000–2006 budget was called the financial perspective. Keep the user's requested **Team Europe**, **EU Delegation**, and **Multiannual Financial Framework** phrasing as conscious satirical anachronisms, not historical claims. [Council on Team Europe](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2020/06/08/team-europe-global-response-to-covid-19-council-welcomes-the-mobilisation-of-almost-36-billion-and-approvesconclusions/), [EEAS on delegation naming](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/azerbaijan/who-we-are_en), [European Commission on historical budgets](https://commission.europa.eu/strategy-and-policy/eu-budget/motion/past_en)
- **Open setting choice:** Bellaluxe is currently the fictional player country yet contributes 17.6% of the EU budget. Recommend treating it as a fictional stand-in occupying one of 25 membership slots, without naming which real country it replaces; the rest of the resident cast can use real EU-country names. Alternative: preserve all 25 historical states plus Bellaluxe as an explicit alternate-history 26th member. Do not claim both a historically exact 25 and an additional fictional member. Decide before fixing the full roster.
- The 17.6% figure is an in-universe premise, not a historical figure or a real country's contribution. The game need not define numerical shares for every state or make those numbers add up until a real gameplay system requires it.

## Naming and tone

- Real country names are allowed for EU member states. Host country Ombrazia, Port Hesperia and non-EU third countries remain invented, with deliberately non-obvious combinations rather than thinly disguised real counterparts. Diplomats are fictional individuals, not caricatures of actual people.
- Satirise diplomatic rituals, conflicting incentives, institutional habits and particular personalities. Give each representative a goal, competence, weakness and private concern; the joke should not require the player to know an insider stereotype.
- Host officials have their own priorities and agency. Some favour powerful bilateral partners or overlook smaller states; others recognise smaller missions, exploit divisions or deal competently with everyone. The pattern is a source of choices, not a single personality for the entire host country.

## The 17.6% running gag

Introduce the premise in dialogue: Bellaluxe contributes 17.6% of the EU's Multiannual Financial Framework. The player repeatedly attempts to turn that contribution into 17.6% of the biscuits, coffee, speaking time, meeting seats, photo space, agenda, credit for projects or blame for failures. Other diplomats react differently: one brings a stopwatch, another points out absent members' interests, the head of delegation proposes a jointly branded biscuit, and a host official invites only the largest bilateral donors.

Use it as a recurring joke, not a literal budget or stopwatch mechanic by default. Offer alternatives: play along, challenge it, use it to advance a real objective, or give a smaller representative time. Outcomes can affect trust, the EU meeting, Capital/host goodwill and a later story. The full premise should be comprehensible in each independent introduction; later callbacks can be shorter.

## Proposed local EU cast

Start with a resident core of about eight representatives including the player, plus an EU Delegation head; grow based on screen readability and story need. These are roles to develop, not fixed names or national assignments. Use individual quirks and avoid making any character merely the sum of a national cliché.

| Role | Comic pressure | Useful capability and potential conflict |
|---|---|---|
| EU Delegation head (develop Celeste) | Promotes "Team Europe" and is quick to place the delegation logo on member-state work. | Can convene partners and unlock joint initiatives; needs member states to do work and resents being bypassed. |
| Large-state ambassador A, potentially France | Guarded about protocol, visibility and bilateral access. | Can reach the host ministry quickly but expects prominent credit. |
| Large-state ambassador B, potentially Germany | Wants a serious, measurable plan, until the meeting is about whose template to use. | Brings capacity and process discipline; may frustrate others by asking for another framework. |
| Player: Bellaluxe | Invokes 17.6% when convenient, then questions the arithmetic when blame is apportioned. | Can broker deals, do the work, compete for credit or defend smaller voices. |
| Nordic representative, potentially Sweden or Denmark | Passionate about inclusion and suspicious of the word "informal" on an invitation. | Offers credible cooperation and can organise smaller missions; has their own political limits. |
| Southern representative, potentially Italy, Spain or Portugal | Treats the formal agenda as a suggestion but takes a slight to their mission seriously. | Can unlock contacts through hospitality; may expect a return invitation. |
| Smaller-state representative A, potentially Luxembourg or Ireland | Frequently mistaken for another country; has a longer institutional memory than everyone else. | Knows procedural history and absent partners' views, but gets tired of being used as a footnote. |
| Smaller-state representative B, potentially Finland or Austria | Represents an absent colleague by prior arrangement and carries two folders with conflicting instructions. | Can gather several views efficiently; may need time or a trade-off before committing them. |
| UK representative, a member in 2006 | Wants a workable common line while publicly reserving the right to disagree with it. | Can lend bilateral access or quietly obstruct a joint statement. |

Not all states have local embassies or attend every meeting. Explicitly author who is present and which absent missions have authorised someone else to speak; a representative should not silently acquire authority over an absent country. An absent mission may send instructions, change its mind or object to being misquoted. Final country assignments and the size of the resident cast need review alongside the Bellaluxe slot.

## Story and location proposals

1. **The budget-share meeting:** An EU coordination meeting allocates biscuits and then, absurdly, speaking time by contributions. Choose whether to claim the full share, share it with an absent smaller mission, challenge the method or support the head's "Team Europe" photo. A later joint statement or bilateral invitation reflects the choice.
2. **The jointly branded project:** The head asks member states to fund and do the work for a host-country project, then proposes an EU-branded announcement. Decide who does the work, whether to agree a credit arrangement, and whether to invite host counterparts into the decision. Pay off at the Minister's visit or in a later meeting.
3. **The representative's two folders:** A resident ambassador speaks for two smaller absent missions with incompatible instructions. Help resolve the wording, exploit the disagreement or delay. A follow-up shows who feels represented and whether the common line survives.
4. **The invitation hierarchy:** A host ministry invites large member states to a consultation and omits smaller ones and the EU Delegation. Decide whether to attend alone, argue for wider representation, share information afterwards or delegate. Each route has costs and consequences for access and trust.
5. **National days and informal diplomacy:** Real EU member-state national-day receptions and coordination coffees join the existing fictional third-country receptions. Attendance, delegation, refusal or silence can alter a named diplomat's trust and open a later interaction. Treat dates and costs as authored game details; avoid claiming a representative can attend two conflicting events.
